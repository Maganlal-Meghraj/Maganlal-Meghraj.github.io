KOSH lending platform

Flexport Capital
Context
Loans ran on Canopy, a third-party system.
Approach
In-house platform on Kotlin/Ktor, GraphQL, PostgreSQL and Temporal; phased migration over a Kafka outbox and Debezium CDC sync.
Result
Two weeks early, 1M+ records, zero integrity incidents, $800K projected savings.
Read the story · moving live loans off Canopy
Problem
Move 1M+ financial records and every merchant off Canopy while loans kept accruing interest and taking payments. There was no window to pause.
Approach
Two phases. Before cutover, cron jobs pulled Canopy data into KOSH. Once a client's writes moved to KOSH, each write also landed in an outbox table in the same transaction; CDC carried it to Kafka, and a consumer replayed it into Canopy. A second CDC stream fed KOSH into Snowflake for drift checks.
Why
Canopy stayed a complete, current copy, so any client could roll back, downstream tools still reading Canopy kept working, and the two systems could run side by side for comparison.
Rejected
Writing through to Canopy synchronously. Canopy's latency was one of the reasons for leaving it, and it would have sat in every KOSH request.
Surprises
Drift checks surfaced a systemic interest/fee mapping error inherited from Canopy across loans, schedules and payments. I fixed it at the data layer and backfilled history once billing risk was ruled out. Canopy's data model also didn't map cleanly onto KOSH's, so the sync needed translation rules.
Result
Clients moved one by one with zero data-integrity incidents, and KOSH launched two weeks early.

FBA orchestration engine

Seller Portal

Approach

Read the story · taming Amazon FBA orchestration
Problem
Sending inventory to Amazon FBA meant a chain of SP-API calls that had grown from 6 to 24. Bursts hit Amazon's rate limits, and a failure partway left shipments stuck: ~25 incidents a week.
Constraint
B2B ran on Lambda with a 30-second limit, so retrying inside the synchronous chain could never fit.
Approach
Each step became a message on a FIFO SQS queue, consumed with long polling at a controlled rate and retried from the step that failed.
Rejected
Step Functions: no global control over the SP-API call rate, a per-transition cost that added up at this volume, and no built-in per-shipment ordering and deduplication like FIFO message groups.
My part
Owned the investigation and the architecture design, then was a major contributor to the backend flows during the build.
Result
1–2 incidents a month, while API volume grew 4x.

Read the story · self-approved claims to auth standard
Problem
There was no separate back-office app, so customers and Ops used the same UI. A UI change showed customers the approve button, and the backend APIs didn't check roles, so customers approved their own claims. It was costing money.
Fix first
Reverted the UI change, then added an explicit role check in the backend and marked the incident stable.
Audit
Worked with Ops to find every self-approved claim: ~$60K in total, of which $53K was recovered.
Fix the class
Created a Cognito user group for the people allowed to approve claims, and added decorators to the central auth library that take the groups allowed to call an API and check the caller's membership. Written to work for any group, not just Billing's.
Trade-off
Service-to-service calls stayed allowed by default, which kept internal callers working during the rollout.
Result
On Platform it became a written cross-team standard, adopted by ~10 services across Billing and Inventory.
